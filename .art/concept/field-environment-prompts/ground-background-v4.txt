Subject: a calm, sunlit grass clearing floor seen from directly above, drawn as cut paper. It is built in two layers and the balance between them is the whole point.

Base layer — broad, soft, irregular regions of slightly different green, like sunlight and shade drifting across a lawn. The regions are large and their boundaries are **soft and gradual**, fading into each other over a distance of several blade-lengths. They are not cut shapes: no hard outline, no crisp cut edge, no jigsaw of solid colour patches. If the base layer alone would read as military camouflage, it is wrong. Keep the difference between the lightest and darkest region small — this is a lawn breathing, not a pattern.

Detail layer — small flat cut-paper grass tufts scattered sparsely on top, each about one fiftieth of the canvas height, three to five blades per tuft, pointing in varied directions. Space them apart so that most of the surface between them is open base-layer green: roughly one tuft per twelfth of the canvas width, irregularly placed, never in rows, never evenly spaced. **The tufts are accents on an open field, not a carpet.** If the surface reads as a dense lawn of packed blades, or as hay, or as a hedge seen from above, it is wrong.

Restrained saturation, mid-to-high value. Nothing muddy, nothing grey, nothing blue-green, nothing neon, nothing olive-drab.
