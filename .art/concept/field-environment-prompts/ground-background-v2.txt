Fill the entire square canvas edge to edge with one continuous opaque grass-field ground texture. Every pixel is grass: no background colour behind it, no magenta key, no transparency, no alpha, no border, no frame, no vignette, and no darkening or lightening toward any edge.

This image is used two ways and must work as both: as the floor texture of a 3D ground plane seen from above, and as a full-screen wallpaper stretched behind menu UI. So it must be **tileable**: the pattern at the left edge continues into the right edge and the pattern at the top edge continues into the bottom edge, with no seam and no discontinuity of colour, shape or value where the edges meet. Every shape that touches an edge is cut there and resumes on the opposite edge.

Viewpoint: straight top-down, looking directly down at flat ground. This is a floor, not a scene. No horizon, no sky, no perspective, no depth, nothing standing up out of the surface, no long shadows — the whole surface sits at one single height.

Subject: a sunlit grass clearing floor in a bright forest, drawn as cut paper. Build it from large overlapping matte paper patches of grass in three value bands — a mid base green, a broad lighter band, and a broad darker band. Each patch is one flat solid colour with a **hard, clean, crisply cut edge**, like a sheet of coloured paper laid over another. The patch outlines are soft and organic in shape, not geometric, but the edges themselves are sharp — no feathering, no blur, no airbrush, no soft-focus blending between patches. Keep the patches large and calm: roughly eight to twelve patches across the width of the canvas, not dozens of small ones.

Accents: scatter a small number of tiny flat cut-paper grass tufts across the surface, each no taller than about one fortieth of the canvas height — small enough to read as texture rather than as objects. Space them far apart and irregularly, never in rows and never clustered into a pattern the eye can lock onto. Do not add fallen leaves; the previous version's leaves read as objects lying on the field rather than as ground texture.

Palette: clean warm yellow-green grass with a little more life than a washed olive. Base hex #79A73E, lighter band hex #94C24C, brightest patch hex #A9D15C, darker band hex #5D8730, deepest patch hex #497026. Restrained saturation, mid-to-high value. Nothing muddy, nothing grey, nothing blue-green, nothing neon.

Render in the 2.5D cut-paper master style: large flat matte shapes, one flat solid colour per shape, hard clean edges, no outer contour line, no ink outline, no smooth 3D shading, no gloss, no specular highlight, no glow, no gradient inside a shape. One soft light source from upper-left — patches read lighter toward the upper-left and darker toward the lower-right, but keep this very gentle, as a slight bias in which value band each patch takes, never as an overall gradient across the image, which would break the tiling.

Keep the overall value range narrow and even so that game units and coloured effects drawn on top stay readable, and so that menu text sits on it comfortably. Contrast belongs to the units, not the floor. No single feature may be large enough, dark enough or bright enough to read as an object, a path, a clearing or a landmark.

Exclude: no text, no frame, no border, no watermark, no logo, no vignette, no characters, no creatures, no trees, no rocks, no fallen leaves, no flowers, no mushrooms, no path, no road, no river, no buildings, no props, no cast shadows, no horizon, no sky, no perspective, no depth-of-field blur, no visible tiling grid lines, no repeated identical motif.
