The attached images are references. The first is the game's master rendering style. The second is the approved wooden plank button already shipped in the game — it is a RAISED plank, lit from the upper left with a light top bevel and a dark underside. Build the OPPOSITE of that: a wooden text input field carved as a RECESSED groove pressed INTO a wood surface, for typing a name or a number.

Fill the entire background with one flat, uniform, fully opaque pure magenta, RGB 255 0 255, hex #FF00FF, edge to edge. The background must be that exact single colour and nothing else: no grey-and-white checkerboard pattern, no white, no grey, no gradient, no texture, no vignette, no ground plane, no cast shadow, no glow or halo bleeding into it. Magenta appears nowhere on the subject itself. Use a square canvas.

Subject: ONE single empty long horizontal wooden input-field groove, seen from the front and very slightly from above. It is straight and level — no arc, no bend, no tilt — and exactly 4 times as wide as it is tall, centered across the middle of the canvas, touching within 30 pixels of the left and right canvas edges, with magenta above and below.

Construction, which matters more than anything else — this must read as PRESSED IN, the exact opposite of the plank button which reads as raised, using the same fixed upper-left light so the inversion is legible by lighting alone:
- Because the light comes from the upper left, a groove pressed into the wood casts its own shadow onto its own top-inner wall and catches light on its own bottom-inner wall. Build it from, top to bottom: a thin dark shadow band in #5D4032 along the top inner edge (the wall the light cannot reach), a wide flat recessed floor in a colour clearly darker and less saturated than the surrounding wood — close to #8A6B4A — running the whole length, and a thin lit lip in #D9A972 along the bottom inner edge (the wall that catches the upper-left light), like a groove cut into a plank.
- Outside the groove, a narrow rim of the surrounding raised wood surface is visible on all four sides in light wood #D9A972, so the groove clearly sits inside a larger wooden panel rather than floating on magenta.
- The long middle two-thirds of the groove has nothing else on it: no knots, no nails, no cracks, no notches, no extra grooves, no grain lines, no one-off facet, on either the recessed floor or the surrounding rim. It must look the same if stretched sideways to any length.
- All of the character is at the two ends, within the outer 12 percent of the width on each side. Each end is hand-cut and chunky: the groove's ends are capped by irregular faceted planes in #C08C56, #A2703F and #5D4032, lit from the upper left, echoing the faceted end caps of the plank button reference. The two ends are not identical copies but have the same size and weight.
- The overall outline stays a simple long rectangle of surrounding wood rim with the groove cut into its middle: no notch, no forked or split ends, no extra holes.

Rendering technique: faceted low-poly papercraft, flat polygonal planes, one flat colour per plane, hard creases between planes, no texture, no wood grain, no noise, no gradients, no gloss, no specular highlight, no outline stroke. It must not look like a smooth rendered 3D bar or a bevel filter; it must look hand-built from cut paper like the plank button reference. Restrained saturation.

Exclude: no text, no letters, no icons, no symbols, no cursor, no rope, no nails, no metal, no frame, no second groove, no ground, no drop shadow onto the background, no watermark. A single empty input groove only.

Use a square canvas without a pixel count. Generate exactly one image. Do not regenerate, resize or recolour it, whatever its size. Copy it to .art/concept/wood-ui-2/input-slot-c-raw.png. Do not write any other file.
